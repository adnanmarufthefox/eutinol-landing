Eutinol Gel: A Synergistic Natural-Active Topical Therapy for Diabetic Wound Healing [cite: 1]

Preclinical evidence and path to clinical validation [cite: 1]

1. Executive Summary

Diabetic foot ulcers (DFUs) are among the most serious complications of diabetes mellitus, driving chronic non-healing wounds, infection, and foot amputations [cite: 1]. Existing standard-of-care dressings manage the wound environment but do not actively correct the underlying biology - persistent oxidative stress, unresolved inflammation, and cellular senescence that keeps diabetic wounds from closing [cite: 1].

This white paper describes Eutinol gel - the flagship product of the Eutinol team - a novel topical formulation combining two naturally occurring bioactive compounds, eugenol (from clove) and quercetin (a flavonoid widely present in fruits and vegetables) [cite: 1]. Preclinical evaluation in cell-based (in vitro) models and a diabetic rat (in vivo) model shows that the two compounds act synergistically: the combination outperforms either compound alone at substantially lower concentrations across four therapeutic pillars - antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anti-senescence, and pro-proliferative activity - and accelerates wound closure in diabetic animals to a level comparable with a widely used commercial hydrogel (Intrasite) [cite: 1].

The findings position Eutinol gel as a promising candidate for a new class of topical diabetic wound therapy that addresses the disease mechanism rather than only the symptom [cite: 1]. Human clinical studies are the next required step to validate efficacy and safety in patients with diabetic foot ulcers [cite: 1].

2. The Unmet Clinical Need

2.1 The scale of the problem

Diabetes is a global health emergency. According to the IDF Diabetes Atlas (11th edition, 2025), 589 million adults aged 20-79 are living with diabetes - approximately 1 in 9 adults worldwide - projected to rise to 853 million by 2050 [cite: 1].

  • An estimated 4-10% of people with diabetes have an active foot ulcer at any time, and lifetime incidence of DFU reaches up to 25-34% [cite: 1].
  • DFUs precede the large majority of diabetes-related foot amputations [cite: 1].
  • Prognosis is sobering: 5-year mortality after a DFU is approximately 30% - comparable to many cancers - and exceeds 70% after major foot amputation [cite: 1].

2.2 Why diabetic wounds fail to heal

Wound healing proceeds through overlapping phases of inflammation, proliferation, and remodelling. In diabetes, this orderly process breaks down [cite: 1]. Hyperglycaemia drives the accumulation of advanced glycation end products (AGEs) and reactive oxygen species (ROS), which induce cellular senescence - the irreversible arrest of cell division [cite: 1]. Senescent cells in turn secrete a senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) - a cocktail of pro-inflammatory cytokines that amplifies inflammation, suppresses fibroblast function, and stalls healing [cite: 1]. The result is a chronic wound stuck in a prolonged inflammatory state: high oxidative stress, elevated inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-a), impaired keratinization and re-epithelialization, and delayed granulation tissue formation [cite: 1].

2.3 Gaps in current care

Guideline-based care for DFUs centres on offloading, debridement, infection control, and modern dressings such as hydrogels [cite: 1]. These are effective at managing the wound bed but are largely passive: they do not directly address the oxidative, inflammatory, and senescence-driven biology that sustains the chronic wound [cite: 1]. There is no single gold-standard therapy, and treatment must be tailored to each wound - leaving a clear, unmet need for therapies that actively promote healing by targeting these underlying mechanisms [cite: 1].

3. The Innovation: Eutinol Gel

Eutinol gel is a topical gel formulation containing eugenol and quercetin - a fixed-dose combination of two natural bioactives selected for their complementary pharmacology [cite: 1]:

  • Eugenol, a phenylpropanoid found abundantly in clove (Syzygium aromaticum), has a long history of topical use as an antiseptic and analgesic agent [cite: 1].
  • Quercetin, a flavonol found in citrus, leafy vegetables, seeds, and nuts, is a well-characterised antioxidant and anti-inflammatory molecule reported to accelerate diabetic wound repair by modulating macrophage behaviour [cite: 1].
  • Both compounds are listed by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) as not classifiable as carcinogenic to humans, supporting a favourable safety profile for topical use [cite: 1].

The formulation rationale is synergy: the two compounds engage complementary and partially overlapping pathways, so a combination may achieve stronger biological effect than either compound alone - potentially at lower doses, with a correspondingly lower risk of irritation or side effects [cite: 1]. Eutinol is the first fixed-dose topical gel to combine eugenol and quercetin for diabetic wound healing - a pioneering combination whose effects were validated in both in vitro and in vivo models [cite: 1]. The gel is designed for once-daily topical application, formulated to be stable, homogeneous, and skin-compatible [cite: 1].

4. Mechanisms of Action: Four Pillars of Wound Healing Activity

Preclinical evaluation in human dermal fibroblasts and RAW264.7 macrophage cells identified four complementary mechanisms [cite: 1]:

4.1 Anti-inflammatory

  • Marked reduction of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-6 and TNF-a in inflamed macrophages - with the combination achieving the strongest suppression at the lowest concentration tested [cite: 1].
  • Downregulation of CXCL8/IL-8 signalling (including its receptors CXCR1/CXCR2), a key driver of sustained inflammatory recruitment in chronic wounds [cite: 1].

4.2 Antioxidant

Significant reduction of hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) - a major reactive oxygen species elevated under diabetic hyperglycaemia - in inflamed cells, with the combination outperforming single compounds [cite: 1].

4.3 Anti-senescence

Reduction of UVB-induced cellular senescence (measured by B-galactosidase staining), countering the AGE- and stress-driven senescence that impairs diabetic wound repair [cite: 1].

4.4 Pro-proliferative and pro-regenerative

  • Enhanced fibroblast proliferation - the cells responsible for depositing extracellular matrix, contracting the wound, and rebuilding the dermis [cite: 1].
  • Accelerated wound closure in an in vitro scratch-wound model, and increased expression of remodelling-associated genes consistent with tissue repair [cite: 1].

Together, these activities create a pro-healing microenvironment: less oxidative damage, lower inflammatory burden, fewer senescent cells, and more active fibroblasts - precisely the deficits that keep diabetic wounds open [cite: 1].

5. Preclinical Evidence

5.1 In vitro: synergy at low concentration

In cell-based assays, eugenol and quercetin each promoted fibroblast proliferation and wound closure on their own [cite: 1]. The combination showed superior effects at a fraction of the dose [cite: 1]:

  • Fibroblast proliferation was enhanced to a similar or greater extent by the combination at ~200 ng/mL as by each compound alone at 6.25 μg/mL - a >30-fold lower active concentration [cite: 1].
  • The combination produced the highest wound closure rate in the scratch-wound model [cite: 1].
  • In inflamed macrophages, the combination reduced H2O2 by ~56% (versus ~24-30% for either compound alone) and cut IL-6 and TNF-a levels substantially more than single-compound treatments [cite: 1].
  • Senescent cell levels were markedly lower with the combination than with either compound alone [cite: 1].

5.2 In vivo: accelerated healing in diabetic rats

In a streptozotocin-induced type 2 diabetic rat model with full-thickness excisional wounds, once-daily topical application of Eutinol gel for 14 days [cite: 1]:

  • Accelerated wound contraction - reaching ~62% by day 14, versus ~47% in untreated diabetic controls, and comparable to the commercial hydrogel comparator (Intrasite) [cite: 1].
  • Improved keratinization and epithelialization - hallmarks of a well-formed, healed epidermal barrier [cite: 1].
  • Supported fibroblast proliferation and granulation tissue formation, with no fibrinoid necrosis observed in treated tissues [cite: 1].
  • Showed no abnormal findings in gross necropsy of vital organs, consistent with a favourable local safety profile [cite: 1].

5.3 Interpretation

The data indicate that eugenol and quercetin in combination modulate oxidative stress, inflammation, and fibroblast activity - the core drivers of diabetic wound chronicity - and translate that biology into faster, more complete wound closure in a diabetic animal model [cite: 1]. Efficacy was comparable to an established commercial hydrogel, with the added value of an active, mechanism-based mode of action rather than passive wound-bed management [cite: 1].

6. Significance and Positioning

  • Mechanism-based, not just wound-bed management: Eutinol gel actively targets the oxidative-inflammatory-senescent triad that sustains diabetic wounds, complementing standard dressings [cite: 1].
  • Dose-sparing synergy: Because the combination works at very low concentrations, the formulation may achieve efficacy with a lower risk of local irritation - an important consideration for fragile, chronic wound tissue [cite: 1].
  • Natural, established safety profile: Both actives are naturally occurring and are listed as not classifiable as carcinogenic to humans by IARC; no abnormal organ findings were observed in the animal study [cite: 1].
  • Practical topical format: A stable gel intended for simple once-daily application, compatible with existing wound-care workflows [cite: 1].
  • Accessibility potential: Both active ingredients are abundant natural products, offering a cost-effective path to formulation compared with growth-factor or cell-based therapies [cite: 1].

7. Next Steps: The Road to Clinical Validation

Preclinical promise must be confirmed in humans [cite: 1]. The pathway ahead includes:

  • Clinical safety and efficacy studies in patients with diabetic foot ulcers, building on the existing animal data and formulation characterisation [cite: 1].
  • Regulatory engagement to define the development and registration pathway for a topical wound-care therapeutic in target markets [cite: 1].
  • Scale-up and manufacturing of the gel under pharmaceutical-grade quality systems (GMP), preserving batch-to-batch consistency and stability [cite: 1].
  • Health-economic assessment to quantify the value case - reduced healing times, fewer infections, and fewer amputations - against the cost of DFU care [cite: 1].
